# The Laws of the Fair: Marketing That's Worked for 10,000 Years

Before websites, before print ads, before writing itself — there were fairs. Mobile markets traveling from village to village. The same rules that made cave people stop at a trader's stall still work today.

I call them the **Laws of the Fair**.

## Law #1: You Have the Right to Demand Attention

In a fair with 50 traders, if you don't grab attention, you don't exist. Same online. Microsoft's 2024 study: average attention span is 8.25 seconds. Less than a goldfish.

**The hierarchy:**
- Beginners ask for 5 seconds
- Competent marketers earn 1 minute
- Masters hold attention for hours

Your job: use every psychological mechanism available. Specific numbers, emotional triggers, pattern breaks.

**Example:**
- Bad: "New contact finder tool" → 12% open rate
- Good: "I found 847 valid emails in 3 minutes" → 34% open rate

You're not being pushy. You're being professional.

## Law #2: Take the First Step

Every transaction starts with action. Not planning. **Doing.**

- Planning: "I'll write a blog post"
- Action: Published at 9:47 AM Tuesday

Harvard Business Review 2023: founders who acted within 48 hours were 3.2x more likely to succeed than planners.

Planning feels good but doesn't work. Your brain gets dopamine without risk.

**The 48-hour rule:** When you spot an opportunity, act within 48 hours. Not perfect action. Any action.

## Law #3: Be Visible

If people can't see you, they can't buy from you.

**Visibility hierarchy:**
1. Movement (evolutionary survival)
2. Brightness/color
3. Size
4. Sound

Video gets 1200% more shares than text. Orange buttons convert 32% better than blue. Large CTAs get 47% more clicks.

You can't fight biology. Use it.

## The 10,000-Year Hack

Your first customer must be you. Use your product daily. Find bugs. Fix them. Then find customer #2.

Journal of Marketing 2024: founders who used their product daily were 4.7x more likely to achieve product-market fit.

## When You're Stuck

Ask yourself:
1. Am I demanding attention?
2. Am I taking action?
3. Am I visible?
4. Am I my own customer?

If "no" to any — that's your problem. Not your product. Your execution.

These laws worked for 10,000 years. They'll work for the next 10,000.
